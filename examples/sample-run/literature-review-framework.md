# Literature Review Framework — AI Tools and Learning Habits

Produced by `literature-reviewer` (Stage 3) from the topic brief. This is a
framework: it structures the review and marks where verified sources must be
inserted. It deliberately contains no invented citations.

## Theme 1 — How students actually use generative AI (→ RQ1)

- **Main debate:** whether student use is dominated by answer-seeking
  (shortcut behaviour) or by study-support uses such as explanation,
  summarisation and practice questions — verify the balance reported in
  current survey studies. [source needed]
- **Established:** adoption among undergraduates is widespread and recent;
  usage concentrates around assignment-related tasks. [source needed]
- **Gap:** many surveys count "use" as a single behaviour and do not break it
  down by study activity.
- **This thesis:** measures use per activity (planning, note-taking,
  problem-solving, revision, help-seeking), giving the breakdown the gap
  calls for.

## Theme 2 — AI use and changes in study behaviour (→ RQ2)

- **Main debate:** cognitive-offloading concerns (delegating thinking weakens
  habits) versus scaffolding claims (AI feedback strengthens planning and
  revision habits) — present both positions before evaluating. [source needed]
- **Established:** self-reported habit change is the most common evidence
  type in this young field; longitudinal behavioural evidence is scarce.
  [source needed]
- **Gap:** few studies separate habits that students say changed from habits
  they value changing.
- **This thesis:** asks about direction of change per habit, not a single
  overall judgement.

## Theme 3 — Student perceptions and concerns (→ RQ3)

- **Main debate:** whether students experience AI primarily as support or as
  a source of anxiety about skill loss and integrity boundaries.
  [source needed]
- **Established:** perceptions are mixed and correlate with how the tool is
  used, not merely how often. [source needed]
- **Gap:** perception studies rarely connect perceptions back to the
  specific habits in Theme 2.
- **This thesis:** links perception questions to the same five habits used
  throughout, keeping RQ2 and RQ3 directly comparable.
