# Topic Brief — The Impact of AI Tools on College Students' Learning Habits

Produced by `topic-analyst` (Stage 1) and confirmed by the student.
Topic slug: `ai-tools-learning-habits`

## Refined topic statement

This thesis investigates how the regular use of generative AI tools (such as
chat-based assistants) is associated with changes in the learning habits of
undergraduate students, and how students themselves perceive that relationship.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Undergraduate students at a single university.
- Self-reported learning habits: study planning, note-taking, problem-solving
  approach, revision behaviour, and help-seeking.
- Generative AI tools used for study purposes.

**Out of scope**

- High-school or postgraduate populations.
- Measured academic performance (grades) as an outcome variable.
- Detection of AI-generated coursework or academic-misconduct policy.

## Research questions

- **RQ1** — How frequently, and for which study activities, do undergraduate
  students use generative AI tools?
- **RQ2** — Which learning habits do students report having changed since
  they began using AI tools, and in what direction?
- **RQ3** — How do students perceive the effect of AI tools on their own
  learning, and what concerns, if any, do they express?

## Key terms

- **Generative AI tool** — a chat-based or embedded AI assistant that
  produces text responses to user prompts, used by the student for study.
- **Learning habit** — a recurring, self-reported study behaviour (planning,
  note-taking, problem-solving, revision, help-seeking).
- **Perceived effect** — the student's own judgement of whether and how a
  habit has changed, as distinct from a measured behavioural change.
